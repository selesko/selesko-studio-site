## The Name

"Selesko" is not just my middle name; it is my grandmother's maiden name. To carry the name Selesko is an act of bringing the past forward. It is a reminder that we do not build in a vacuum; we build in a continuum. Just as a home inherits the landscape it sits on, our work inherits the stories that came before. It is a commitment to craft architecture that honors where we come from while sheltering where we are going.

## What the Studio Does

Selesko Studio operates at the intersection of architecture, objects, and ideas. The work divides into three paths:

**The Collection.** Limited-run design studies — fully resolved concepts ready for fabrication. Architecture concepts, lamp designs, furniture. These are not mood boards; they are thought experiments made buildable.

**The Custom Commission.** A single project, designed specifically for a person and a place, following a rigorous 4-step process. For buildings: *Discovery & Feasibility → Concept & Simulation → Virtual Construction → Stewardship.* For objects: *Discovery & Context → Form & Study → Tectonic Detail → Fabrication & Patina.*

**Field Notes.** The intellectual practice of the studio. Writing about time and adaptability, the honest use of materials, the climate of the Pacific Northwest, the ritual of daily life in space. These are not blog posts. They are position papers — a record of how I understand the built environment.

## Design Philosophy

**Transparency first.** A successful project is built on clear communication and predictable steps. We use digital tools to keep clients informed from the first sketch to the final punch list.

**Build it twice.** Before we build in the field, we build digitally. BIM resolves the conflicts — how the mechanical systems fit within the floor structure, how the air-sealing plane turns a corner — so we don't solve them in the rain on the job site.

**Protect the vision.** A drawing is only as good as its execution. During construction, the studio acts as the client's advocate — reviewing submittals, answering RFIs, visiting the site at critical junctures to ensure high-performance details are actually built.

**Time and adaptability.** Materials age. Buildings settle. The question is whether they do so gracefully. The weathered steel, the patinated brass, the shou sugi ban — these are materials that earn their age. We don't design for the day of completion; we design for thirty years after it.

**The joint is where the design lives.** How does the white oak meet the blackened steel? How is the stone anchored without visible fasteners? How does the LED warm the grain of the wood above it? Beauty is in the assembly. The tectonic detail is not a footnote — it is the argument.

**Passive performance as baseline.** Every building should work with its climate, not against it. Passive House principles — super-insulation, thermal bridge elimination, controlled mechanical ventilation, airtight construction — are not premium add-ons. They are the starting point.

## The Site as Publishing Platform

The selesko.studio website is not a portfolio in the traditional sense. It is a record of thinking — design studies that may never be built, field notes that document how I understand the built environment, object concepts that explore proportion and material at 1:1 scale.

The goal is to populate this record continuously. Not just with finished work, but with the *process* of thinking through a design problem. The concepts — SF.001, SF.003, SF.04 "Scoria House", C.002, C.004, L.001 through L.011 — are study notes as much as they are proposals. Each one tests a hypothesis: about site, about material, about the relationship between a building and its landscape.

The field notes are the connective tissue. They explain the thinking behind the work: why glass boxes fail in the Pacific Northwest, why sourcing local matters beyond the marketing, why mass timber is a structural argument as much as an environmental one.

## The Agentic Studio

The studio has already mapped a conceptual fleet of 10 specialized AI agents for the practice:

| Agent | Phase | Role |
|---|---|---|
| The Intake Interrogator | Pre-Design | Extracts Owner's Project Requirements from transcripts |
| The Zoning Analyst | Feasibility | Defines the 3D buildable envelope from code |
| The Narrative Weaver | Concept | Writes the architectural story / parti |
| The Image Prompter | Visualization | Generates Midjourney / DALL-E prompts |
| The Code Consultant | Design Development | IRC / IBC code citations |
| The Building Scientist | Details | Water, air, and thermal control |
| The Material Curator | Interiors | Finishes, palettes, tactile description |
| The Spec Writer | Construction Docs | 3-Part CSI specifications |
| The Permit Wrangler | QA/QC | Pre-submittal checklists |
| The CA Administrator | Construction | RFI responses, liability protection |

Originally mapped as Gemini Gems, these are now being rebuilt natively with Claude and the Claude Agent SDK. The next evolution is end-to-end pipeline automation:

1. Jeff drafts a concept brief in Notion
2. Claude (Narrative Weaver) generates the architectural story and description
3. Claude (Image Prompter) generates visualization prompts
4. Claude builds the HTML project page using the established site templates
5. Claude pushes to GitHub → Vercel auto-deploys

One command from concept brief to live page. The website becomes a continuously updated record of the studio's thinking rather than a static portfolio updated by hand.
